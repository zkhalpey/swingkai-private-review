Evidence layer

Science behind the cue.

Tim’s coaching concepts are separated into supported principles, coach hypotheses and questions that still require measurement.

Measurement concept

Peaks, timing and variability

Illustrative only. Real analysis needs repeated trials, declared events and synchronised instrumentation.

Ground interaction is measurable

Supported principle

Ground-reaction forces and centre-of-pressure movement are measurable features of the golf swing. One universal “correct” path is not established.

Systematic review · PMID 41653371 ↗
Product rule: use a validated pressure or force system before showing force-derived values.

Foot tripod as a coaching cue

Coach hypothesis

The heel, first-metatarsal and fifth-metatarsal tripod is a useful visual model. SwingKai does not claim that maintaining one pressure distribution is optimal for every golfer.

Continuous pressure strategies · PMID 37149899 ↗
Test against instrumented traces, shot outcome, comfort and between-golfer variability.

Kinematic sequence needs repeated trials

Supported principle

Segment timing can be studied, but single frames and vendor efficiency percentages cannot define a universal swing model.

Kinematics systematic review · PMID 35736831 ↗
Report curves, event definitions, variability and measurement error rather than one score.

Practice cues should preserve learning

Supported principle

Short structured practice, an external focus and appropriate variability can support motor learning. Adherence and transfer still need prospective testing.

Motor learning systematic review · PMID 38419912 ↗
Pilot completion, comprehension and retention without converting self-report into biomechanics.
Scientific references support the measurement and learning framework. They do not validate Tim’s entire method or establish clinical, injury-prevention or handicap-improvement claims.
